## Solid black and extreme contrast

Blackwork uses only black ink and areas of bare skin. At SUBA TATTOO, a tattoo studio in Montcada i Reixac (Barcelona), we approach it with dense blocks, controlled textures and compositions with strong visual weight. The deepest areas are packed in solid black and the transitions are built with stippling or open textures, avoiding the greyish veil that appears when the pigment is diluted too much.

## Pieces with graphic presence

Every design aims for a firm, recognisable silhouette. 'Iron Beetle', on the outer thigh, borrows the language of an old engraving: strong symmetry, volume through chiaroscuro and a central shell that organises the whole piece. In 'Japanese Peony', the traditional motif is translated into stippling densities that create depth without relying on colour.

Blackwork is not drawn on flat skin. The figure follows the curve of the arm or leg so that movement strengthens the composition, and the outlines are refined so the piece reads clearly even from a distance.

## A style that ages with dignity

Well-saturated black is one of the pigments that best withstands the passing of time. To make sure it does, the composition leaves breathing room between solid masses and the edges open up gradually. After the session, aftercare is the same as for any tattoo: washing with neutral soap, applying balm in thin layers and protecting the piece from the sun.
